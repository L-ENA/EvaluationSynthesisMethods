## 3. Executive summary 

## **Background** 

Information, Education, and Communication (IEC) is a process of adequately supporting information and education to people to advance healthy behaviors which are influenced by several factors such as the local context, family, and the community – factors that are crucial to an individual’s growth[1] . 

The concept of Social and Behavior Change Communication (SBCC) which is an evidence-based, consultative process of addressing social attitudes, knowledge and practices has evolved from earlier models of IEC done through identifying, analyzing and providing communities and individuals with relevant information through welldefined strategies. SBCC aims to make changes in knowledge, attitudes, and practices and has emerged as a more comprehensive approach than IEC. While IEC involves use of wide range of channels and material to pass on fact based instructional messages to the individual or community, SBCC acknowledges underlying socio-economic factors that affect individual behavior and, uses robust approach to facilitate improved social behavior and mobilize targeted populations for change. Various countries and international organizations across the globe have begun investing in SBCC activities to empower individuals and communities, and lower structural barriers that hinder people from adopting positive practices and societies from becoming more equitable, inclusive, cohesive and peaceful.[2] 

Over last two decades, India’s growth has contributed phenomenally to global human development. However, despite making considerable progress over the years, it continues to face challenges with reproductive health, child health and nutrition.[3] SBCC interventions have increasingly been seen as crucial contributor towards addressing social and cultural barriers in health programmes and enhancing positive behavior towards health and wellbeing of oneself and community, as a whole.  In this light, UNICEF has been working actively along with Ministry of Health and Family Welfare (MoHFW) and state governments to strengthen the health system institutional structures for SBCC. Behavior change communication has been inculcated as a part of the larger Health Systems Strengthening program approach. 

In 2013, a national behavior change communication (BCC) strategy for RI was developed with UNICEF’s support. UNICEF also extended its support to GoI towards institutionalization of SBCC Cells and transformed communication focus from IEC to demand generation through behavioral change. In 2014, Global Alliance for Vaccines and Immunization (GAVI) provided support to this by revamping and revitalizing existing IEC/BCC institutions; or setting up new ones within the state institutions. The SBCC Cells were equipped with trained human resources and budgets that directly supported the State Immunization Division and played a significant role in planning and operationalization of communication interventions for flagship interventions such a s Mission Indra Dhanush and new vaccine introductions. Under the GAVI HSS 2 UNICEF is mandated to engage and facilitate the process of SBCC Cells with the sustainability considerations as stated in the programme document, which outlines that the cells must be fully state government owned and operated. 

## **Objective:** 

With the GAVI programme gradually drawing to a close, this UNICEF SBCC evaluation is intended to provide critical programmatic insights into what has worked, what has not worked and key learnings from the sample states included in the evaluation. Similarly, the findings of the evaluation are expected to support strengthening of SBCC cell functioning, and ongoing program initiatives aimed directly at maximizing utilization of public services.  Based on the findings of the evaluation, UNICEF aims to advise the MoHFW and other public administrative bodies on the effectiveness and sustainability of institutionalizing SBCC Cells. 

## **Methodology:** 

PricewaterhouseCoopers India (PwC) was engaged by the UNICEF India Country Office (ICO) to undertake this evaluation study. The geographic scope of the evaluation state specific (six states, namely, Odisha, Madhya Pradesh, West Bengal, Uttar Pradesh, Telangana, and Assam). A purposive sampling approach was used in district selection across sample states. Further, details on the rationale used for district selection has been included in the methodology section of the report (3.4.2). The SBCC Cells were assessed on the OECD DAC criteria to understand

## **Key findings** 

The main findings are described for each evaluation criterion and structured around the key evaluation questions that guided the evaluation. 

**Relevance** : The SBCC Cells aligned with the national and state priorities and other programmes, however the degree of alignment varied from model to model. The cells-initiated activities enhanced the reach and adherence of the programmes and interventions at grass root level. Even during the pandemic cells responded pro-actively and developed strategies, designed materials and planned activities to ensure right information is provided to people in efficient and effective manner. 

**Effectiveness** : SBCC cells have been only partially successful in achieving their desired goals and objectives. Lack of systematic structure, scarcity of financial and human resources, limited scope of creativity negatively impacted the performance of the cells. Although the cells were established to lead the communication change interventions but majority of them ended up being supportive unit to the state’s IEC activities. 

**Efficiency:** SBCC cells across the states (except Telangana) did not have a say in allocation of financial resources for the SBCC activities. This limits the cells’ efficiency in delivery due to lack of adequate funding for monitoring, research and evidence generation and other essential aspects of SBCC. Due to various reasons like vacant posts, retired / transferred personnel, lack of dedicated communication cadre etc. coupled with lack of dedicated financial allocations leads to reduced efficiency of cells to function within planned timelines. 

**Coherence** : In most of the states, the cells had good coordination and convergence with state government. Cells are used to consistently provide government support for implementation of communication strategies at state and district level. However, intersectoral convergence was missing due to which cell were not able to achieve its goals and objectives in effective and efficient manner. It was also observed that cells failed to utilize the potential of other development partners and NGOs in creating a difference and initiating social behavioral change. 

**Sustainability** : The SBCC cells work is well integrated into the programming of the departments they are housed under. However, they have a considerable scope of scaling up and integration in the public health system of the country and states. The government (at the state and district-level) have been reported to have limited capacity to adequately carry out the activities of the SBCC Cells independently at present due to limited technical knowledge / expertise and dedicated human resource and financial allocations. Collaborative efforts of all stakeholders would enhance the pool of resources and increase the reach of the cells and help in its transition. 

**Conclusio:** The SBCC cells program was initiated to help state government departments to transition from IEC to SBCC. Though this transition is not yet complete for any of the six states evaluated in this study, the inception of the cells has got the ball rolling in the right direction. The state and district level officials are now familiar with the term ‘SBCC’ and are aware of its basic conceptual definition and how it differs from IEC. They are also aware of the novel methods being used in SBCC such as miking, interpersonal communication, leveraging key influencers, etc. The SBCC program has contributed to health systems strengthening in these six states by bringing about a change in the way health, ICDS and PRI officials approach and engage with community members. 

In conclusion, it can be said that the SBCC cells program **kickstarted the transition of communications activities from IEC to SBCC** . The program's contribution has been significant and in many of the states been the first SBCC intervention initiated. Though the progress has been slow over the past programmatic cycle, it is important to understand that to bring about a change not just in methods but in the approach, understanding and attitude towards communications is a mammoth task. The very principles of SBCC can be applied here to see the slow progress not as a sign of inefficiency and ineffectiveness of the program, but rather as a **work in progress status for the cells** . The cells require guidance and support for at least one more programmatic cycle whilst they figure out their transition strategy to move from being UNICEF supported to completely government owned. **We strongly suggest UNICEF to continue its investment** and support in the program during this period.

## **Recommendations:** 

The recommendations have been formulated based on the findings which were analyzed through primary and secondary data. The recommendations are divided between those which are common to all the cells across all 3 categories and those which are specific to each category, and are formulated specific to the two main stakeholders, i.e., the government and UNICEF. Broadly, the recommendations are focused on addressing the need for establishment of district-level cells in all the six states, continued need of technical assistance and aid in handover process from UNICEF, capacity building and skill development of health officials at the state, district and block level, and the need for increased agency for cells to lead SBCC programming and have a greater say in budget and timeline planning. We also recommend, a similar evaluation to assess the progress of the transition of the state and decide future course of action at the end of the next programmatic cycle of five years.

## Introduction 4. 

India emerged as world’s fifth largest economy by Nominal GDP and third largest by PPP in the year 2022[4] . Along with development of extensive tangible infrastructures there has been emphasis on change and progress at grass root level. Social building blocks and interventions in the form of Sarva Shiksha Abhiyan for increasing access to education, midday meal for nutritious food to children, Mission Indra Dhanush for Routine Immunization (RI), comprehensive primary care through Health and Wellness Centers (HWCs) and other measures for ensuring maternal and child health have been implemented to ensure a community where people are healthy (not merely illness free) and take onus of their health and wellbeing. In health sector, India has made substantial progress over the past decades. It is evident from the fact that, life expectancy at birth in India has grown from 45 years in 1960s to 70 currently.[5 ] 

India is presently in a state of demographic and epidemiological transition. It bears triple burden of diseases namely, the infectious diseases; the noncommunicable diseases (NCDs) linked with lifestyle changes; and emerging epidemics and pandemics[6] . Despite making considerable progress over the years, it continues to face challenges with reproductive health, child health and nutrition[7] . Indicators, like, Maternal Mortality Ratio in India being just below 100[8] , Infant Mortality Rate being 28 and Under 5 Mortality rate being 32[9] suggest that India still has a long way to go to achieve SDG targets[10] . It is essential to understand that better health and wellbeing of individuals cannot be established only through development of curative health services. Various interrelated social and behavioral determinants of health that contribute towards morbidities and mortalities need to be taken into consideration as they pose tougher challenges to the public health system. In fact, the epidemiological transition is fueled by these social and behavioral determinants of health in addition to other risk factors. 

Nutrition is a crucial factor for health and development. Similarly, it is fundamental for improvement in infant, child and maternal health. It also leads to stronger immune systems and contributes to safer pregnancy and childbirth and lowers the risk of non-communicable diseases[11] . Over the years, India continues to face a double burden of nutritional problem. It can be seen through NFHS-5 data that, there is sub-optimal progress in health and nutritional status in the population. In 2019 –21, only 11% of breast-feeding children aged 6 - 23 months (about 2 years) receive an adequate diet, which was 8.3% in 2015-16 (NFHS 4). On the other hand, among the non - breastfeeding children the number has gone down to 12.7%, from 14.3% in 2015-16. When we consider children aged under 5 years, 35.5% are stunted (height-for-age), 32.1% are underweight (weight-for-age) and 19.3% are wasted (weight-for-height). Additionally, percentage of anemic children among children aged 6-59 months (about 5 years) has increased from 59% to 67% between 2015-16 to 2019-21. Malnutrition and anemia in young age sustains throughout the life span of individuals, especially women. Among the women aged between 15-19 years, 59% are anemic and among pregnant women aged 15-49 years 52.2% are anemic. On the other hand, obesity is also increasing its prevalence in India. According to NFHS – 5 data, 24% women and 23% of men in India are overweight or obese. Additionally, 57% women and 48% men have high risk waist-to-hip ratio[12] . The double burden of high prevalence of undernourishment and increasing prevalence of obesity in a country reiterates the need for social and behavior change interventions. 

The Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs), in particular, SDG 3 – Good health and well-being, and SGD 5 – Gender Equality cannot be achieved without bringing in behavioural change at all levels, combined with structural policy implementation. While practitioners of SBCC in India at state and district levels are tackling various challenges related to health (maternal, infant, and child mortality, etc.,), gender biases and equity and participatory approaches of practitioners, SBCC representatives on field and within communities, they can engage more effectively for partnerships through greater resource utilization and wider impact of SBCC interventions.
