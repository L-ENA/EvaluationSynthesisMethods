## 3. Executive summary 

## **Background** 

Information, Education, and Communication (IEC) is a process of adequately supporting information and education to people to advance healthy behaviors which are influenced by several factors such as the local context, family, and the community – factors that are crucial to an individual’s growth[1] . 

The concept of Social and Behavior Change Communication (SBCC) which is an evidence-based, consultative process of addressing social attitudes, knowledge and practices has evolved from earlier models of IEC done through identifying, analyzing and providing communities and individuals with relevant information through welldefined strategies. SBCC aims to make changes in knowledge, attitudes, and practices and has emerged as a more comprehensive approach than IEC. While IEC involves use of wide range of channels and material to pass on fact based instructional messages to the individual or community, SBCC acknowledges underlying socio-economic factors that affect individual behavior and, uses robust approach to facilitate improved social behavior and mobilize targeted populations for change. Various countries and international organizations across the globe have begun investing in SBCC activities to empower individuals and communities, and lower structural barriers that hinder people from adopting positive practices and societies from becoming more equitable, inclusive, cohesive and peaceful.[2] 

Over last two decades, India’s growth has contributed phenomenally to global human development. However, despite making considerable progress over the years, it continues to face challenges with reproductive health, child health and nutrition.[3] SBCC interventions have increasingly been seen as crucial contributor towards addressing social and cultural barriers in health programmes and enhancing positive behavior towards health and wellbeing of oneself and community, as a whole.  In this light, UNICEF has been working actively along with Ministry of Health and Family Welfare (MoHFW) and state governments to strengthen the health system institutional structures for SBCC. Behavior change communication has been inculcated as a part of the larger Health Systems Strengthening program approach. 

In 2013, a national behavior change communication (BCC) strategy for RI was developed with UNICEF’s support. UNICEF also extended its support to GoI towards institutionalization of SBCC Cells and transformed communication focus from IEC to demand generation through behavioral change. In 2014, Global Alliance for Vaccines and Immunization (GAVI) provided support to this by revamping and revitalizing existing IEC/BCC institutions; or setting up new ones within the state institutions. The SBCC Cells were equipped with trained human resources and budgets that directly supported the State Immunization Division and played a significant role in planning and operationalization of communication interventions for flagship interventions such a s Mission Indra Dhanush and new vaccine introductions. Under the GAVI HSS 2 UNICEF is mandated to engage and facilitate the process of SBCC Cells with the sustainability considerations as stated in the programme document, which outlines that the cells must be fully state government owned and operated. 

## **Objective:** 

With the GAVI programme gradually drawing to a close, this UNICEF SBCC evaluation is intended to provide critical programmatic insights into what has worked, what has not worked and key learnings from the sample states included in the evaluation. Similarly, the findings of the evaluation are expected to support strengthening of SBCC cell functioning, and ongoing program initiatives aimed directly at maximizing utilization of public services.  Based on the findings of the evaluation, UNICEF aims to advise the MoHFW and other public administrative bodies on the effectiveness and sustainability of institutionalizing SBCC Cells.

A purposive sampling approach was used in district selection across sample states. Further, details on the rationale used for district selection has been included in the methodology section of the report (3.4.2). ## **Key findings** 

The main findings are described for each evaluation criterion and structured around the key evaluation questions that guided the evaluation. 

**Relevance** : The SBCC Cells aligned with the national and state priorities and other programmes, however the degree of alignment varied from model to model. The cells-initiated activities enhanced the reach and adherence of the programmes and interventions at grass root level. Even during the pandemic cells responded pro-actively and developed strategies, designed materials and planned activities to ensure right information is provided to people in efficient and effective manner. 

**Effectiveness** : SBCC cells have been only partially successful in achieving their desired goals and objectives. Lack of systematic structure, scarcity of financial and human resources, limited scope of creativity negatively impacted the performance of the cells. Although the cells were established to lead the communication change interventions but majority of them ended up being supportive unit to the state’s IEC activities. 

**Efficiency:** SBCC cells across the states (except Telangana) did not have a say in allocation of financial resources for the SBCC activities. This limits the cells’ efficiency in delivery due to lack of adequate funding for monitoring, research and evidence generation and other essential aspects of SBCC. Due to various reasons like vacant posts, retired / transferred personnel, lack of dedicated communication cadre etc. coupled with lack of dedicated financial allocations leads to reduced efficiency of cells to function within planned timelines. 

**Coherence** : In most of the states, the cells had good coordination and convergence with state government. Cells are used to consistently provide government support for implementation of communication strategies at state and district level. However, intersectoral convergence was missing due to which cell were not able to achieve its goals and objectives in effective and efficient manner. It was also observed that cells failed to utilize the potential of other development partners and NGOs in creating a difference and initiating social behavioral change. 

**Sustainability** : The SBCC cells work is well integrated into the programming of the departments they are housed under. However, they have a considerable scope of scaling up and integration in the public health system of the country and states. The government (at the state and district-level) have been reported to have limited capacity to adequately carry out the activities of the SBCC Cells independently at present due to limited technical knowledge / expertise and dedicated human resource and financial allocations. Collaborative efforts of all stakeholders would enhance the pool of resources and increase the reach of the cells and help in its transition. 

**Conclusio:** The SBCC cells program was initiated to help state government departments to transition from IEC to SBCC. Though this transition is not yet complete for any of the six states evaluated in this study, the inception of the cells has got the ball rolling in the right direction. The state and district level officials are now familiar with the term ‘SBCC’ and are aware of its basic conceptual definition and how it differs from IEC. They are also aware of the novel methods being used in SBCC such as miking, interpersonal communication, leveraging key influencers, etc. The SBCC program has contributed to health systems strengthening in these six states by bringing about a change in the way health, ICDS and PRI officials approach and engage with community members. 

In conclusion, it can be said that the SBCC cells program **kickstarted the transition of communications activities from IEC to SBCC** . The program's contribution has been significant and in many of the states been the first SBCC intervention initiated. Though the progress has been slow over the past programmatic cycle, it is important to understand that to bring about a change not just in methods but in the approach, understanding and attitude towards communications is a mammoth task. The very principles of SBCC can be applied here to see the slow progress not as a sign of inefficiency and ineffectiveness of the program, but rather as a **work in progress status for the cells** . The cells require guidance and support for at least one more programmatic cycle whilst they figure out their transition strategy to move from being UNICEF supported to completely government owned. **We strongly suggest UNICEF to continue its investment** and support in the program during this period.

## **Recommendations:** 

The recommendations have been formulated based on the findings which were analyzed through primary and secondary data. The recommendations are divided between those which are common to all the cells across all 3 categories and those which are specific to each category, and are formulated specific to the two main stakeholders, i.e., the government and UNICEF. Broadly, the recommendations are focused on addressing the need for establishment of district-level cells in all the six states, continued need of technical assistance and aid in handover process from UNICEF, capacity building and skill development of health officials at the state, district and block level, and the need for increased agency for cells to lead SBCC programming and have a greater say in budget and timeline planning. We also recommend, a similar evaluation to assess the progress of the transition of the state and decide future course of action at the end of the next programmatic cycle of five years.

## 5.1. Conceptual framework: Theory of change 

Based on document review, consultation with UNICEF ICO and UNICEF SBC specialists across the states under focus for this study, a retrospective Theory of Change (ToC) has been developed for the SBCC Cells. The activities of the Cell are categorised under the five pillars of Health System Strengthening including: (1) Leadership and governance; (2) Workforce; (3) Service Delivery; (4) Finance; and (5) Information. The overall intent of the SBCC Cell emerged to provide support for improvement of demand generation and accelerate uptake of government schemes that can positively impact a child’s wellbeing through enhanced and equitable access to government sponsored services/schemes for health, nutrition, education, water, sanitation, and protection. Accordingly, the outcomes for this model are: (1) Sustainable government systems created for SBCC programming; (2) Improved service delivery* and outreach of government programmes; (3) Improved uptake and beneficiary participation towards government sponsored programmes/schemes; and (4) Positive policy environment created for SBCC programming across sectoral development initiatives. 

To attain this intent, the **pathways of change** are designed as following: 

- If the leadership is sensitised on the need for behavioural change, then increased number of programmes will adopt SBCC at the design and result stage 

- If SBCC Cell is institutionalised under the government set-up then SBCC-based programming will be more sustainable, accountable, and transparent 

- If SBCC relevant content is integrated in programme planning and capacity development, then beneficiary identification, targeting, coverage and outreach will improve 

- If IPC capability of functionaries is strengthened, then their convincing power and quality of service delivery will improve 

- If the desired behaviour is established, then communities will actively participate in monitoring service delivery and thereby demand for improved service quality 

**Overarching assumptions:** Availability of a nodal govt department/institutional set up to host the SBCC Cell; adequate resource mobilization by nodal department/institution in supporting competent HR and financial budgets; availability of a network of community mobilizers to support awareness and demand for services. 

## **Risks:** 

- Given the vast socio-cultural diversity, inability to identify behavioural triggers based on social norms and cultural practices 

- If housed within a Directorate, difficulty in convergence and resource pooling 

- If not housed under a Directorate, difficulty in getting participation from functionaries and officers (under other line departments) at the district and community level 

- Restricted budget for mix-media integration 

- Inhibition of shifting from IEC based approach

## 5.3. Sampling design 

## i. Selection of states 

The assessment was planned across **six states** in India across the country’s regions: Assam, West Bengal, Odisha, Jharkhand, Telangana and Madhya Pradesh. The states were selected purposively considering UNICEF’s categorization of high burden states (Madhya Pradesh), tribal (Odisha, North-East), good governance (Telangana). Geographical variance in the eastern region (North-East (Assam), East (Odisha, West Bengal), Central (Madhya Pradesh and Jharkhand) and South (Telangana) was ensured, providing a more representative picture of the program. However, Jharkhand, a sample state for the evaluation, was later replaced with Uttar Pradesh. This change was made considering that the SBCC Cell in Jharkhand has been handed over to the government. prior to the evaluation period. Hence, the evaluation of this cell would not enable assessment of UNICEF’s role in supporting the cell during the evaluation period. 

Evaluation in the states of Odisha, Madhya Pradesh, West Bengal, and Uttar Pradesh was done at two levels as there are two levels of SBCC Cells, at the state and district level. In the remaining two states of, Assam and Telangana, the SBCC Cells only function at the state level, hence the evaluation was carried out at the state level.

## ii. Selection of districts/blocks 

We understand that marginalized communities including scheduled castes, scheduled tribes, religious minorities, and persons with disabilities have been historically excluded and may be under-represented/covered/reported across development programmes intervention focus and results. While there have been efforts towards taking into consideration the need for greater intervention focus, and integration of such stakeholders within active planning and implementation of SBCC and related programming, there are further opportunities no ted in this regard.[45] In this context, a stepwise methodology was used to ensure diversity of population in the selection of districts in the sample states. 

**Step 1:** Within diversity of population, district level values of percentage of scheduled caste population, percentage of scheduled tribe population, percentage of population of religious minorities and total district level population of Persons with Disability was mapped across the sample states. The Census 2011[46] data was considered for the above-mentioned indicators. 

**Step 2:** Two districts per sample state were selected based on values mapped across indicators mentioned above, elaborating the overall diversity of population[47] . One district with a lower than state average value across the selected indicators and another with a higher than state average value across the selected indicators per state was selected. Further, distance from state capital was also considered in the process of district selection.

## 5.4. Data collection methods 

The data was collected through qualitative interview guides/ FGD guide. Pilot testing of both the qualitative and FGD guides was conducted to assess the feasibility of data collection tools in Mandar district of Jharkhand in September- October 2022. After the successful completion of the pilot testing, a team consultation was arranged, and the tools were revised based on the observations from the pilot. The revisions included incorporating new areas of inquiry which emerged during the discussion, and feedback received on the questions framed during the pilot testing. 

The tools were administered by a trained research team, oriented, and trained on the study methodology and technical components of interview/ FGD guides. The study team who conducted the interviews were public health professionals with prior experience of conducting such studies. On field training of the team for conducting the interviews was also carried out. Safety protocols were followed during the data collection to counter the spread of COVID-19 and protect all the individuals associated. 

To ensure the quality of collected data, certain identified aspects were verified through observations such as availability of IEC material (Flyers, posters, media clips on social media), display of IEC material at the SBCC cell, and health centers etc. And those related to routine practices were assessed through observation and record reviews. Similarly, the team corroborated aspects on capacity building by SBCC cells by observation of training like ToT for master resource person and other capacity building sessions. 

The questionnaire was administered in Hindi/ English based on respondents’ preference. FGD guide was administered in local languages taking the help of translators. Translators were provided with an orientation on the study methodology and FGD guide beforehand. The team of researchers was trained to administer the questionnaire in the respective languages. The qualitative interviews were audio-recorded after getting the oral consent of the respondents. In case the consent for audio recordings was not obtained, manual notes were taken for the same.  On average, the interviews took 30 minutes each. The interview was stopped when information saturation was reached i.e., no new information was emerging from the interaction. Data collection in each state took five days of time, by a team of 2-3 researchers. Data was collected during November 2022 to January 2023.

## 5.6. Limitations of the study 

- The use of quantitative and numerical data points pertaining to health, behavioral and social indicators is limited within the scope of this evaluation. A link of causality and/or attributability cannot be formed between said datapoints and findings of the study. The scope of the study is limited to understanding contribution of SBCC cell in health system strengthening. 

- We could not cover the target sample as we could not conduct few stakeholder consultations across state, district and block levels owing to unavailability of relevant stakeholders or prior commitments to other engagements or unresponsiveness to communications shared requesting for consultations. However, the response rate for the study was approximately 90 percent, which is acceptable in social science research. 

- There was limited awareness of SBCC related activities among both trainers, trainees of SBCC and community members across a few sites. Further, the recently transferred recruitments had limited institutional memory on the evolution of the SBCC Cell in the state. Limited relevant insights were received on the nature of work done and challenges faced in doing so and evolution of SBCC cell through the interactions conducted with the above stakeholder groups. 

- There is a lack of any prior in-depth, comprehensive baseline studies that could offer insights into all dimensions of SBCC programming, that could further be used or referred to during the evaluation. Hence, it was observed that the gaps in covering and strengthening SBCC programmes in certain areas in each state was largely attributed to the absence of on-ground research and profiling based on demographic and socioeconomic status of the populations in the respective settlements. 

- There was lack of institutional memory within the government related to SBCC cell including relevant documentation which might have resulted in recall bias. The evaluation team tried to include appropriate probes in the questionnaire to aid respondents’ recall of activities. - As there is no state level SBCC Cell in Uttar Pradesh, the stakeholders mapped at the state level were from different IEC cells across line departments with limited technical context and awareness of SBCC related activities. As a result, the input received from such consultations highlighted limited relevant insights on the same. 

- In Assam, West Karbi, Anglong was one of the proposed evaluation districts initially. However, we had  to change the district to Morigaon due to conflict in the district. 

- The good practices that we have documented in the report have been compiled by information received from the respondents and we have not validated the same through field observations.

## **My Village, My Home campaign** 

The Maternal and Child Health Integrated Program (MCHIP) adopted a community-level tool-based programme called My Village, My Home. The objective of this tool is to strengthen service delivery and RI coverage for communities, assess the high drop-out rates and to set up a system of tracking beneficiaries in districts, and to improve the quality of coverage. FLWs use this visual depiction of the immunization status of infants and make use of the community to monitor the immunization coverage of all infants in the locality. The tool is designed in a way that the information on the oldest infant in the community of the respective village is on the bottom row, while that of the younger infants are in the rows upward. The dates of vaccination are written or highlighted in colours in the respective boxes against the names. The advantage of the tool is that it has increased intersectoral collaboration and is a community-friendly tool that can be accessed easily by parents of children, and the ASHA, Anganwadi and ANM workers.

## **Conclusion** 

The cells across all three categories were found to be lacking in three out of the four short term outputs mentioned in the theory of change. The cells were not successfully able to establish systems and processes to design, implement and monitor SBCC plans/programs and use evidence for programme planning hence leading to limited evidence, documentation, and completed studies on SBCC. Though it was found that effective dissemination for SBCC program evidence through workshops, media, etc was being taken place. Hence, it can be concluded that within the evaluation criterion of effectiveness, the cells have a major scope of improvement to achieve the first and fourth longer term outcomes in the theory of change namely, institutionalization of SBCC programming through sustainable systemic reforms, and positive policy environment created for SBCC programming across sectoral development initiatives. 

## 6.3. Efficiency 

Efficiency measures the extent of resource utilization in the optimum, economical and timely manner. It provides justification about resources utilized in the achievement of intervention’s goals. Evaluation of efficiency of an intervention includes critical evaluation of timeliness, economic and operational efficiency. Throughout the SBCC cell evaluation, programme was examined through cell’s functioning within allocated resources and within planned timelines. 

The efficiency of the cell was analysed through insights received by interviewing state, UNICEF consultants (loaned staff) and district level officials of SBCC cells.

## **Category 1** 

This has been partially achieved. ## 6.6. Gender and Equity 

From the six evaluated states, it was found that although gender and equity are cross-cutting and a critical component of the SBCC, in practice, the Cells in the states have not yet mainstreamed gender equity. Neither are the Cells gender-cognizant while designing SBCC activities, as was noted under Relevance and Effectiveness across all the categories. ## Conclusion 7. 

The SBCC cells program was initiated to help state government departments to transition from IEC to SBCC. Though this transition is not yet complete for any of the six states evaluated in this study, the inception of the cells has got the ball rolling in the right direction. The state and district level officials are now familiar with the term ‘SBCC’ and are aware of its basic conceptual definition and how it differs from IEC. They are also aware of the novel methods being used in SBCC such as miking, interpersonal communication, leveraging key influencers, etc. The SBCC program has contributed to health systems strengthening in these six states by bringing about a change in the way health, ICDS and PRI officials approach and engage with community members. 

It can be said that the program is operating in alignment with all four desired outcomes mentioned in the theory of change . Evidence suggests that the cells are contributing and moving towards outcomes one and four which are creation of sustainable government systems for SBCC programming and creation of positive policy environment for SBCC programming across sectoral development initiatives, respectively by achieving, to varying degree, some of the shorter term output goals such as having leadership and officials trained on SBCC methods, and establishing systems and processes to design and implement SBCC programs. The scope of this evaluation does not include assessing outcome two and three which are namely improved demand and outreach of government programs and improved uptake and community participation towards government sponsored programs/schemes. 

It was found that the model of cell in Telangana (CRU) under category 3 was functioning in a manner most efficient to integrate SBCC programming in state planning and implementation of initiatives. As opposed to all the other cells, CRU has managed to establish itself as one stop shop for all communications activities in the state which allows the direction of pursuit for CRU to be such that the departments approach the cell for support out of their own understanding of the significance of having a communications strategy for their respective programs. In this way, CRU has been able to market itself as a center of technical expertise in communications which supports various departments in developing communications strategies. Further, it was found that CRU’s mandate to only operate at the state level acts as an advantage as its resources can be focused on strategizing, whilst implementation remains within the ambit of the state departments. This also ensures that the cell is able to function effectively in the role of providing technical expertise on communication within a smaller pool of human resources and budget allocation. It is important to note that though CRU’s model works best, it is still heavily dependent on UNICEF for its budget allocation. For the sustainability of the cell, CRU should advocate with NIRDPR for fund allocation from within the department’s budget. 

The SBCC cells’ functioning was found to have high **relevance** and alignment with the national and state priorities by contributing to building the communications strategy for flagship programs of departments under which they are housed. The cells have been seen to be flexible and adaptable to evolving needs in the community such as the incidence of Covid-19 pandemic. Further, to varying degrees, the cells have been able to leverage existing systems and institutions of service delivery such as conduction of on field implementation through the cadres of frontline health workers; and have been able to have the presence and guidance of senior government officials in creating the **leadership and governance** structure of the cell.  But, due to various limitations such as inadequate **finance** , challenging timelines, a **workforce** which does not specialize in communications or behavior change and lack of inter-departmental convergence, the **effectiveness** and **efficiency** of the **service delivery** by the cell has been negatively affected. The cells have been only partially successful in fulfilling their role as institutions of evidence generation, monitoring and evaluation, documentation and analysis. Although the cells were established to bring about a change in the way communications is planned, programmed and implemented, a majority of them ended up playing the role of a supportive unit to the state’s IEC activities. The aspects that the cells are working to successfully deliver in are training and capacity building, strategic planning, knowledge sharing and increasing awareness of community members by disseminating health **information** and positive social practices. 

Most of the cells have been able to foster an environment of convergence and collaboration with relevant departments and with other development actors such as IDAs, NGOs and CSOs. Though it is important to note that the need for even higher convergence (especially intersectoral convergence such as a collaboration between health and education department) was noted from the findings across all six states. Through these collaborations, the cells are able to have a higher contribution to integrating SBCC into state programming. Hence, it can be concluded that the cells have **coherence** with institutions and interventions of health and related fields, but not intersectoral. 

The cells have a considerable scope of scaling up and being fully integrated in the public health system of the country. However, to ensure the program’s **sustainability** , government ownership and accountability of the cells

is of utmost importance. The first step in this process would be for the government to have a complete shift from IEC to SBCC in its attitude and approach. Following which, the government must conduct regular capacity development workshops and training in communications and behavior change methods for its officials who also play the role of communications officers. This would improve the quality of SBCC material being developed, lead to better planning and programming resulting in more technically sound communications strategies. 

It was found that although **gender and equity** are cross-cutting and a critical component of the SBCC, in practice, the Cells in the states have not yet mainstreamed gender and equity, and neither are there any gender focused programme beyond a basic programmatic focus on gender within health programs. For example, focus on gender in programs such as Poshan Abhiyan and RMNCHA+. Though, through the programmatic focus on gender and equity within health programs, vulnerable populations such as young others from lower socio-economic strata and those in LODOR communities are included in the state programming. 

In conclusion, it can be said that the SBCC cells program **kickstarted the transition of communications activities from IEC to SBCC** . The program's contribution has been significant and in many of the states been the first SBCC intervention initiated. Though the progress has been slow over the past programmatic cycle, it is important to understand that to bring about a change not just in methods but in the approach, understanding and attitude towards communications is a mammoth task. The very principles of SBCC can be applied here to see the slow progress not as a sign of inefficiency and ineffectiveness of the program, but rather as a **work in progress status for the cells** . ## 8. Policy implications and recommendations 

The objective of this evaluation exercise was to understand the SBCC Cells’ performance towards maximizing utilization of allocated resources and augmenting the results of the health systems strengthening initiatives and other cross sectoral themes of social and health determinants at the state and district level. A qualitative research design was adopted for the same. Findings from this study have provided insights into the status of the existing SBCC cells and identified certain areas for improvement in its functioning. 

The suggestive recommendations for strengthening the functioning of SBCC cells are **based on the findings from study, field observations, consultations with stakeholders.** 

Confidence in the generalizability of the findings and recommendations is enhanced by the comprehensive strategy and design used for the **selection of the states and arena of stakeholders interviewed as a part of this evaluation** .

## **Action points for State:** 

- ➢ The state SBCC cell should include monitoring and evaluation, as crucial pillar of the SBCC cell for the state. For which the state should instate a domain expert for M and E who can guide other officials for ensuring regular monitoring of SBCC activities in the state. 

- ➢ The state should conduct periodic monitoring and evaluation of the activities of SBCC activities through field visits, community observation etc. 

- ➢ The state should invest in leveraging technology (dashboard, apps etc.) to monitor SBCC activities. 

## **Action points for UNICEF:** 

- ➢ UNICEF should design a sustainable monitoring and evaluation framework involving input and process indicators, like availability of SBCC strategy, percentage of fund utilization, number of training, trainee/trainer feedback etc., for states to follow and adopt in long run. 

- ➢ UNICEF should also act an independent evaluator of the performance of the cells across the states. 

- ➢ UNICEF should train and build capacity of the state officials to conduct monitoring and evaluation activities independently.

63

- From the six evaluated states, it was found that although gender and equity are cross-cutting and a critical component of the SBCC, in practice, the Cells in the states have not yet mainstreamed gender equity, and neither are they gender-cognizant while designing SBCC activities, as was noted under relevance and effectiveness across all categories. WASH services and RMNCH+A has given attention to hygiene and sanitation, nutrition and women’s health. 

- However, SBCC activities are largely limited to comprehensive health strategy under RMNCH+A with priority intervention for nutrition, IFA supplementation, and so on. Wide gaps persist in fundamental areas of adolescent sexual and reproductive health (SRH), information and counseling, and in early marriage, teen pregnancy, school dropout of girls, and even male child preference in some states. In this context, the Rashtriya Kishor Swastha Abhijan and ARSH (national programmes for adolescent sexual and reproductive health (SRH) and holistic development) were not featured, neither mentioned by the states during the consultations). 

- As was found across the six evaluated states, there are no (in-depth) gender equity integrated SBCC activities for health for women and men, and adolescent boys and girls. There are no participatory studies related to sexual and reproductive health. Therefore, SBCC activities should emphasize the importance of health priorities but more comprehensively that intersects with related fundamental social issues, central to gender and equity. 

- Furthermore, it was found that the departments are not yet gender-focused, and largely do not have the necessary skills or level of knowledge to initiate gender and equity programmes, except for Odisha which has a relatively better equitable approach to SBCC planning. 

- The evaluation further found that a comprehensive Gender and Equity approach was missing at the state level SBCC agenda, while no specific gender-focused programmes are part of the district cell’s SBCC activities. A gender-transformative approach is thus a critical requirement to address social norms, practices in diverse social and economic contexts by the state and district cells, and assessing areas that need genderbased intervention, such as those in LODOR and other vulnerable communities. In view of this, UNICEF should support to the states in creating an enabling environment that is gender responsive on priority within its next programmatic cycle/ as a part of its transition strategy. Presence of a ‘Gender and Equity aware’ program staff will enable assessment of contexts and consciously address existing gaps, gender constraints, and so on within SBCC. 

- We gather that UNICEF’s support and interventions will be imperative both at state and district levels to initiate a dialogue and consolidate the SBCC agenda on mainstreaming Gender and Equity more thematically within the Cells. To ensure relevant, focused and effective delivery of the SBCC objectives, UNICEF should support state and district cells on research-based inputs on gender responsiveness and inclusivity and create the initial dialogue between states and districts for a Gender Equality Continuum.
